Three Styles, Now in 3D

Same three directions, rebuilt as live 3D dioramas. These are rough primitive-shape mock-ups to judge look and lighting, not final art. Move your mouse over a scene to shift the camera; use the buttons to change station or zoom to a carriage.

3/4 dollhouse cameraRaised, slightly angled view into an open-fronted carriage, like the cutaway and isometric-room references, instead of a flat side-on elevation.
Real depth and parallaxTrees, ruins and buildings sit at true distances behind the windows and scroll past, so nearer things move faster. Fog gives the layered haze from your forest references.
Real lamp lightWarm lamps and a soft top light cast actual light and shadows across furniture, figures and rugs, like the glowing room render.
Three rendering looksA: matte paper cut-outs. B: toon shading with ink outlines. C: soft rounded miniature with glow and tilt-shift.

Updated recommendation

Going 3D changes the cost picture. Style B now looks the closest to your references, and toon shading with outlines means you can get that look from simple 3D shapes instead of hand-inking every prop, so it is far cheaper than before. Style A remains the lightest on performance and the best for readability. Style C is the most inviting but the most likely to fight the horror mood.

My pick: B's toon-and-outline carriages and lighting, with A's flat-colour silhouette scenery. Tell me which direction you like and I'll write it up as a proper style guide.
