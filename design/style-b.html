The four stations

The train

Streamlined deco locomotiveA 1930s bullet-nosed steam engine and coal tender at the head of the train: bottle-green casing with brass bands, a brass-on-oxblood The Last Train nameplate, a headlamp casting a beam down the line, and steam from the chimney. The cab is an open cutaway like the carriages, with the driver and a glowing firebox.
Working running gearLarge oxblood driving wheels with spokes, counterweights and crank pins. The coupling rod, connecting rod and piston move as the wheels turn, with a small leading and trailing truck.
Proper wheels and bogiesEvery carriage now rides on two bogies of spoked, flanged steel wheels, with side frames, brass axle boxes, leaf and coil springs, and brake shoes. Buffers, end steps and bellows gangways join the carriages.
1930s fashion-plate passengersSlimmer, taller figures built from your 1930s fashion references: drape-cut shoulders, nipped waists, wide trousers with turn-ups, bias-cut gowns, real lapels, ties, belts and buttons, plus fabric patterns (pinstripe, tweed, argyle) and props.

How the scenery is built

7–8 painted depth layersEach station is a stack of painted panels placed at real distances: far peaks or skyline, a haze band, a mid treeline, near trunks, undergrowth, and a foreground strip in front of the train.
Atmospheric perspectiveFar layers are painted lighter, bluer and lower-contrast; near ones darker and sharper. That is what makes the forest refs read as deep rather than flat.
One light per stationA low sun from the right (forest), a pink moon behind the swamp, fire glow from below (city), cold moonlight from the left (snow). Rim light is painted on the side facing it, and the carriage lighting is tinted to match.
Living detailsLight shafts, drifting mist, flickering fire layers, smoke plumes, mirrored reflections on the flood water and wet ash, embers, fireflies and falling snow.
