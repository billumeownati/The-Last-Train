The Last Train · Art Direction

Three Initial Styles

All three keep your core idea — a side-on lamp-lit cutaway train against scrolling painted scenery that darkens at each station. They differ in how the world is drawn, which changes mood, effort, and how well it reads when six people are staring at it while arguing.

What I took from your 14 references

Cutaway train interiorsCross-section, side-view and isometric room refs. Dense props (globes, typewriters, gramophones), brass art-deco trim, warm cel-shaded light, heavy ink outline. This is your "dollhouse" feeling.
Window-silhouette gridThe strongest idea for a social game: lit rectangles in a dark body, each person a flat silhouette. Instantly readable, and every carriage window can be a "room".
Layered sceneryForest silhouettes, purple flooded swamp, burning village, overgrown skyline. Atmospheric depth through value steps; blue-to-orange gradients; a different palette per station.
1930s fashion platesCloche hats, fedoras, bowlers, flat caps, long coats, wide trousers. Silhouette variety comes from hat + coat length, so characters stay distinct even tiny.
Ticket and UI refsSepia engraved ticket with perforation and stamps; a paper-card social-deduction board with red string; a soft rounded cozy panel UI. These map to three different UI directions.
The tension in your refsSome are cozy and rounded, some are noir and inked, some are flat and graphic. Picking one lane per style gives a cohesive result.

My recommendation

Start with Style A (Paper Lamplight), and borrow Style B's ticket-and-stamp UI. A's flat shapes are cheap to produce and easy to animate and parallax in a browser. The silhouettes also make "who is where" readable at a glance. Because the Stowaway is unaware and the story darkens, reducing each station to a limited palette does the storytelling for you.

Style B is the most beautiful per frame but costs the most art, and hand-inked detail fights with parallax and moving figures. Style C is the friendliest, but its cosiness can undercut the dread at stations 3 and 4, unless you lean on that contrast deliberately.
